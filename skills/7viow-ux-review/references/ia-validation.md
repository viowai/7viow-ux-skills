# Validate organization and finding paths

Use for classification, navigation, labels and findability. Select evidence for the actual question and keep one finding per underlying problem. Review does not change product sources.

## Expert task walkthrough

Start from a real reader goal and supplied content. Identify what the reader knows, where they enter, the decision they need to make, and the expected result. Walk the actual interface where presentation or behavior matters. A site map establishes hierarchy; it cannot prove task flow, rendering or interactive recovery.

Check whether the organization follows recognizable objects, tasks, time or facets. Look for overlapping categories, misleading labels, lost content, contradictory metadata and grouping that forces the reader to remember a previous page. Judge cognitive burden through the actual decision and content, not a universal number of items.

Trace entry → finding → detail/evidence → action → return. A page reachable through search, a deep link, contextual navigation or a valid role entrance is not an orphan merely because it is absent from the main menu. Report a dead end when the promised reader cannot proceed or recover. A 200 response alone does not prove useful reachability.

When content moves, compare the affected inventory with actual destinations, queries and exports. Distinguish a removed duplicate from a lost unique fact. An internal field is not automatically reader content; missing optional metadata need not appear, while missing decision-critical information must remain honest.

Use task-walkthrough.md for filter/sort combinations, counts, qualified identity, state and return behavior. Use review-rubric.md for visual grouping and readable conditions. Completion: a finding identifies an actual task failure, its evidence, minimum repair and observable recheck; a working path may pass.

## Tree testing and participant evidence

When a tree test is requested, separate the tested hierarchy from visual navigation. Prepare representative finding tasks without naming the target menu label in the question; define intended destinations and acceptable alternatives. Record participants, environment, first choices, paths, completion, backtracking and limitations when those observations exist.

Without participants, deliver a test plan or clearly labeled expert walkthrough. Do not invent participant counts, timings, success rates or a validated mental model. Tree-test success does not validate the rendered product's controls, visibility or recovery. A study conclusion needs its actual observed data, not a completed plan document.

## Existing analytics

Only analyze available authorized data. Confirm event definition, counted object, period, sample/denominator and relevant segments before inferring a finding problem. Repeated visits may reflect successful reuse or confusion; low traffic may reflect a rare but essential task. Frame explanations as testable hypotheses and connect them to observed paths. Do not install tracking or export data by implication.

For formal statistics, retrieval ranking or knowledge-model questions, request the relevant specialist evidence and retain the UX question. A technical metric does not establish that a reader understood the result.

## Locale, channels and iteration

For relevant languages/channels, check recognizable terms, meaning, state continuity and the actual rendered expansion. Equivalent tasks may use different layouts; identical labels can still be culturally ambiguous. Tone must preserve action, state and necessary conditions. Distinguish expert language judgment from actual participant research.

After a repair, rerun the affected finding task, representative alternate entrance and relevant adjacent state against the new version. Verify source/regeneration evidence when shared data produces several views. Keep unresolved and untested findings visible; do not require a new complete research program for a bounded fix.
