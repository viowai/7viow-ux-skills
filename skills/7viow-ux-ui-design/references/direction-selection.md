# Direction generation and selection

Read when a task involves a new core page/keyframe, substantial layout redesign or information-architecture reorganization and its direction remains undecided. This is a core design stage. Reuse a selection already supplied by the user; a local repair stays inside that decision unless new evidence invalidates it.

## Establish the decision

Define the reader's task and page intent: the primary object/decision, next action, expected outcome and return/recovery. Derive a classification/finding model and information priorities before drawing. Assign decision-critical, supporting, background and optional content to visible, deferred or removed roles with reasons.

## Produce 2–3 substantive alternatives

Use [direction-options-template.md](../assets/direction-options-template.md). Each option must show:

- A distinct strategy for the same user need, expressed through information grouping/order, finding path, primary action and interaction. Explain meaningful layout and visual-hierarchy differences. Color or decoration changes alone do not form another option.
- Representative supplied content, meaningful proportions, primary/secondary emphasis and the important state or transition. A native design frame, lightweight HTML keyframe or another requested visual medium is suitable; prose or indistinguishable boxes alone cannot support a visual choice.
- The desktop/narrow-screen behavior relevant to this task, including what stays visible, collapses or moves, and how the next action remains findable.
- A failure check: where does a new reader look first, can they understand the page's purpose, can they find the next action, and does secondary material compete with it?

Compare usability, information communication, interaction effort, extensibility and implementation risk. Recommend an option with reasons and describe any useful combination; do not hide the weaker option's real tradeoffs or fabricate user-test results.

## Select, then develop

Present the actual alternatives and ask one decision question: choose a direction, combine named parts, or request another strategy. Continue independent fact/state analysis while waiting. Do not start full implementation, high-fidelity polishing or a final artifact before the consequential direction is selected, unless the user has explicitly delegated that decision.

If selection is delegated, record the authorization, compare the alternatives and explain the selected direction. For historical replay, use the actual recorded decision at its original point in the sequence; do not invent a favorable user response.

Preserve the selection across later turns and repairs. Reopen only the specific decision challenged by new evidence, explaining what changed. Completion: 2–3 inspectable strategies, a meaningful tradeoff comparison, and a recorded user/delegated choice lead into development.
